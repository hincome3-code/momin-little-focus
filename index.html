Why black and white

Newborns see strong contrast first.

Bold edges

In the first weeks, a baby's eyesight is blurry and colours are hard to tell apart. Sharp black-and-white shapes are the easiest things to find and follow.

Then red

Red is one of the first colours babies pick out. Our Fruits & Red set adds it to apples, cherries, zigzags and diamonds for the months ahead.

Time together

The cards are made for you to hold, move and talk about. Each design has its name printed under it, so you always know what to say.

How to use

A few calm minutes, many times a day.

  1. Hold it closeShow one card 20–30 cm from your baby's face while they are calm and awake.
  2. Move it slowlyGlide the card from side to side and say its name.
  3. Prop it up for tummy timeStand a few cards in front of your baby to give them something to lift their head for.
  4. Follow your babyStop when they look away or fuss, and try again later. Short is good.

Questions

Good to know

From what age can we use the cards?

From the first weeks. Start with the simple black-and-white patterns and faces, then bring in the Fruits & Red set as your baby grows.

How long should each session be?

A few minutes is plenty. Watch your baby: when they look away, yawn or fuss, they have had enough for now.

Why are there names on the cards?

So you can talk about each picture. Hearing your voice name what they are looking at is part of the play.

Can my baby hold or chew them?

No. The cards are for you to hold and show. Keep them out of reach when you are not playing together, and put away any card that gets damaged.

Does it make a good gift?

Yes. The boxed set is a popular pick for baby showers, naming ceremonies and first visits.
